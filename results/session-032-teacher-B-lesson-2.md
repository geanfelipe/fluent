## Good
- **Improvised role-play** with a clear structure: problem → solution (one-hour meetings + written action items) → risks (people need to adjust) → success metric (more time for their own tasks) → AI to speed things up. The teacher said he "understood pretty much everything" and that the learner "worked on the fly pretty well".
- Reused new forms at once: *the best in the whole country*, *astute*, *approached*, *get used to this new approach*, *count on*.
- Precise questions: "Why 'crafty' fox?" · "Is there a word in English like 'astucious'?" · "Is the goat 'he' or 'it'?"
- Emphatic *do*: "I **do** think this approach is good."

## Coach notes
- Animals are **it** by default and **he/she** in stories or for pets.
- **Listening plan** (for the learner's processing problem): the teacher explains for 2 min, the learner summarizes for 2 min and asks 2 questions. Metacognitive listening (predict → listen → check → reflect) improves comprehension (Vandergrift & Tafaghodtari 2010). Vocabulary also matters: you need to know about 95–98% of the words to follow speech (van Zeeland & Schmitt 2013).
- **Balance:** cap the story at ~15 min so the role-play gets ≥25 min.
